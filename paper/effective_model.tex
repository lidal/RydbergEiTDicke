\title{\bfseries From four levels to the Dicke model\\[3pt]
\large a step-by-step reduction for cavity Rydberg-EIT electrometry}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-8mm}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
We reduce the four-level Rydberg-EIT ladder in a cavity to a collective spin--boson model,
in three moves: adiabatic elimination of the intermediate state, rotation into the
microwave-dressed Rydberg basis, and a Holstein--Primakoff expansion for $N$ atoms. Each step
is a matrix operation and each carries a validity condition, stated as we go. The punchline is
that the microwave dressing leaves \emph{two} coupled branches, not one, so the natural object
is a two-branch Dicke model; the familiar two-level Dicke model is a limit of it, valid only
when one branch is far off resonance. Throughout $\hbar=1$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The system}

Four atomic levels, in the order we will keep them:
\[
  \ket{1}=\ket{g}=\ket{5S_{1/2}},\quad
  \ket{2}=\ket{e}=\ket{5P_{3/2}},\quad
  \ket{3}=\ket{r}=\ket{nD},\quad
  \ket{4}=\ket{r'}=\ket{n'P}.
\]
Three fields act. The \emph{cavity mode} $\hat a$ drives $\ket{1}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{2}$
with single-atom coupling $g_0$; a classical \emph{control} laser drives
$\ket{2}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{3}$ with Rabi frequency $\Om_c$; and the \emph{microwave field
to be measured} drives $\ket{3}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{4}$ with $\Omu = dE_\mu/\hbar$.

Go to the frame rotating at the drive frequencies and make the rotating-wave approximation.
With $\Delta$ the one-photon detuning of the cavity from $\ket{1}\!\to\!\ket{2}$, $\delta$ the
two-photon detuning of $\ket{1}\!\to\!\ket{3}$, and the microwave on resonance, the
single-atom Hamiltonian in the basis $(\ket{1},\ket{2},\ket{3},\ket{4})$ is
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm at} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0            & g_0\hat a\dg & 0            & 0 \\[2pt]
    g_0\hat a    & -\Delta      & \Om_c/2      & 0 \\[2pt]
    0            & \Om_c/2      & \delta       & \Omu/2 \\[2pt]
    0            & 0            & \Omu/2       & \delta
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  H = \omega_c\,\hat a\dg\hat a + H_{\rm at}.
  \label{eq:H4}
\end{equation}
$\omega_c$ is the cavity detuning in this frame --- an effective frequency, freely tunable,
not an optical one. Note the structure: the matrix is \emph{tridiagonal}, because each field
connects only neighbouring levels.

\section{Step 1: eliminate the intermediate state}

The excited state $\ket{2}$ is short-lived and we never want it populated. Detune far from it,
\begin{equation}
  |\Delta| \;\gg\; \Om_c,\;\; g_0\sqrt{\langle \hat a\dg \hat a\rangle},\;\; \Gamma_e ,
  \tag{C1}
\end{equation}
so that its amplitude follows the others adiabatically. Setting $\dot c_2=0$ in
$i\dot c_2 = g_0\hat a\,c_1 - \Delta c_2 + (\Om_c/2)c_3$ gives
\begin{equation}
  c_2 \simeq \frac{1}{\Delta}\Bigl(g_0 \hat a\,c_1 + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}c_3\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Substituting back and keeping the terms generated in $\{\ket{1},\ket{3},\ket{4}\}$:
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm at}^{(3)} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \dfrac{g_0^2\,\hat a\dg\hat a}{\Delta} & \lambda\,\hat a\dg & 0 \\[9pt]
    \lambda\,\hat a & \delta + \dfrac{\Om_c^2}{4\Delta} & \Omu/2 \\[9pt]
    0 & \Omu/2 & \delta
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \boxed{\ \lambda = \frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\ }
  \label{eq:H3}
\end{equation}
Three things were produced. The off-diagonal $\lambda\hat a$ is the \emph{two-photon Raman
coupling} $\ket{1}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{3}$ we want. The diagonal entries are light shifts:
$\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ on $\ket{3}$, which we absorb by redefining
$\delta \to \delta + \Om_c^2/4\Delta$, and $g_0^2\hat a\dg\hat a/\Delta$ on $\ket{1}$, a
photon-number-dependent (Kerr) shift which is negligible for
$g_0^2\langle\hat a\dg\hat a\rangle \ll |\Delta|\,\omega_c$ and which we drop.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Practical warning.} Both $\lambda$ and the light shift $\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ scale with the
control-laser intensity. The shift adds directly to $\delta$, which is the same channel the
measurand uses --- so control-laser intensity noise is indistinguishable from signal. This is
the dominant technical systematic of the scheme, not a detail.
\end{keybox}

\section{Step 2: rotate into the dressed Rydberg basis}

The lower-right $2\times2$ block of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3} is the microwave problem alone:
\begin{equation}
  H_\mu = \begin{pmatrix} \delta & \Omu/2 \\ \Omu/2 & \delta \end{pmatrix}
  = \delta\,\mathbf{1} + \frac{\Omu}{2}\sigma_x .
\end{equation}
Diagonalise it with the orthogonal rotation
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \ket{\pm} = \frac{\ket{3}\pm\ket{4}}{\sqrt2},
  \qquad
  R\dg H_\mu R = \begin{pmatrix} \omega_+ & 0\\ 0 & \omega_-\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \omega_\pm = \delta \pm \frac{\Omu}{2}\ }
  \label{eq:branches}
\end{equation}
These are the Autler--Townes doublet: \emph{the measurand has become a pair of level
positions}. Now apply the same rotation to the whole $3\times3$ matrix. The only subtlety is
what happens to the Raman coupling, which connects $\ket{1}$ to the \emph{bare} $\ket{3}$:
\begin{equation}
  \ket{3} = \frac{\ket{+}+\ket{-}}{\sqrt2}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \lambda\,\hat a\,\ket{3}\bra{1}
  = \frac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\,\hat a\,\bigl(\ket{+}\bra{1} + \ket{-}\bra{1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
In the basis $(\ket{g},\ket{+},\ket{-})$,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  H_{\rm at}^{(3)} =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0 & \dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\hat a\dg & \dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\hat a\dg \\[10pt]
    \dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\hat a & \omega_+ & 0 \\[10pt]
    \dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt2}\hat a & 0 & \omega_-
  \end{pmatrix}\;}
  \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
This matrix is the heart of the reduction, and it says three things worth stating plainly:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Both} dressed states couple to $\ket{g}$ --- there is no basis in which only
        one does.
  \item They couple \textbf{equally}, each with $\lambda/\sqrt2$. The dressing is symmetric;
        nothing here distinguishes $\ket{+}$ from $\ket{-}$.
  \item There is \textbf{no} $\ket{+}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{-}$ entry. That transition is the
        microwave one and carries no cavity matrix element.
\end{enumerate}
The asymmetry that eventually appears comes only from $\delta$, through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:branches}, and from which state the atoms are actually prepared in.

\section{Step 3: many atoms}

Take $N$ atoms, all initially in $\ket{g}$, and work at low excitation,
\begin{equation}
  n_+ + n_- \ll N .
  \tag{C2}
\end{equation}
Holstein--Primakoff then replaces each collective excitation by a boson: $\ket{g}$ becomes the
vacuum, and $b_\pm\dg$ creates one atom in $\ket{\pm}$, spread coherently over the ensemble.
The couplings add constructively, so $\lambda \to \lambda\sqrt N$. Writing
\begin{equation}
  G \equiv \lambda\sqrt{N} = \frac{g_0\Om_c\sqrt N}{2\Delta},
\end{equation}
and keeping for now only the co-rotating terms that Eq.~\eqref{eq:core} supplies,
\begin{equation}
  H = \omega_c \hat a\dg\hat a + \omega_+ b_+\dg b_+ + \omega_- b_-\dg b_-
      + \frac{G}{\sqrt2}\bigl(\hat a\dg b_+ + \hat a\dg b_- + \text{h.c.}\bigr).
  \label{eq:TC2}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:TC2} conserves the total excitation number, so starting from
$\ket{g,0}$ nothing happens: it is an exact eigenstate. To obtain a Dicke model one must add
counter-rotating terms $\hat a\dg b_\pm\dg + \text{h.c.}$, which is done by driving with two
tones (or one modulated tone) so that a second Raman channel creates a cavity photon
\emph{and} excites the atom. With both present,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  H = \omega_c \hat a\dg\hat a + \sum_{s=\pm}\Bigl[\omega_s b_s\dg b_s
      + \frac{G}{\sqrt2}\bigl(\hat a+\hat a\dg\bigr)\bigl(b_s+b_s\dg\bigr)\Bigr]\;}
  \label{eq:dicke2}
\end{equation}
--- a Dicke model with \emph{two} atomic modes.

\section{Step 4: the threshold}

Write Eq.~\eqref{eq:dicke2} in quadratures $x_j=(o_j+o_j\dg)/\sqrt2$. The potential energy is
$\tfrac12\,\bm{x}^{\!\top} V \bm{x}$ with $\bm{x}=(x_a,x_+,x_-)$ and
\begin{equation}
  V = \begin{pmatrix}
        \omega_c & \sqrt2\,G & \sqrt2\,G \\
        \sqrt2\,G & \omega_+ & 0 \\
        \sqrt2\,G & 0 & \omega_-
      \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The normal phase (all atoms in $\ket{g}$, cavity dark) is stable while $V$ is positive
definite. It loses stability when $\det V=0$, and using $\omega_++\omega_-=2\delta$,
\begin{equation}
  \det V = \omega_c\,\omega_+\omega_- - 2G^2(\omega_++\omega_-)
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\ \omega_c\,\omega_+\omega_- = 4G^2\delta\ }
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
This is the general result. Note that positive definiteness also requires $\omega_->0$: if the
lower dressed state drops below $\ket{g}$, the normal phase fails for a mundane reason --- a
two-photon resonance --- and not by symmetry breaking.

\subsection*{The two-level limit}

Suppose the microwave splitting is large, so that one branch is far off resonance:
\begin{equation}
  \Omu \;\gg\; \omega_-,\;\; G,\;\; \omega_c
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \omega_+ \simeq 2\delta \gg \omega_- .
  \tag{C3}
\end{equation}
Then $4G^2\delta \simeq 2G^2\omega_+$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:threshold} collapses to
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \omega_c\,\omega_- = 2G^2 \ }
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  G_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{G}{\sqrt2},\quad
  G_{\rm eff}^{\rm c} = \tfrac12\sqrt{\omega_c\,\omega_-},
\end{equation}
which is exactly the textbook two-level Dicke threshold for a spin with splitting $\omega_-$
coupled at $G/\sqrt2$. \emph{This} is the effective two-level model: the levels are $\ket{g}$
and the \emph{near-resonant} dressed state, and the coupling carries a factor $1/\sqrt2$ from
the dressing. Dropping the far branch without the $1/\sqrt2$, or building the spin on the far
branch instead of the near one, both give wrong answers.

\section{Summary of the reduction}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.0cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.7cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{7.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{What it buys, and what breaks without it}\\
\midrule
Rotating frame + RWA & optical frequencies $\gg$ all Rabi rates & Eq.~\eqref{eq:H4};
  $\omega_c,\delta$ become tunable detunings, not optical frequencies\\
Eliminate $\ket{2}$ & $|\Delta|\gg\Om_c,\,g_0\sqrt{\bar n},\,\Gamma_e$ &
  Raman coupling $\lambda=g_0\Om_c/2\Delta$; leaves light shifts, one of which is the dominant
  systematic\\
Dress $\ket{3},\ket{4}$ & microwave near resonance &
  $\omega_\pm=\delta\pm\Omu/2$: the measurand becomes a level position\\
Holstein--Primakoff & $n_++n_-\ll N$ &
  collective enhancement $\lambda\to G=\lambda\sqrt N$\\
Add counter-rotating & second drive tone &
  without it, excitation number is conserved and $\ket{g,0}$ never evolves\\
Drop the far branch & $\Omu\gg\omega_-,G,\omega_c$ &
  the two-level Dicke model, coupling $G/\sqrt2$, spin splitting $\omega_-$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{2mm}
\noindent The chain of reasoning is: four levels $\to$ (eliminate $\ket{e}$) $\to$ three levels
$\to$ (dress) $\to$ $\ket{g}$ plus two branches $\to$ (many atoms, counter-rotating terms)
$\to$ two-branch Dicke $\to$ (one branch far detuned) $\to$ two-level Dicke. The last arrow is
the only one that is optional, and it is the one most easily got wrong.
