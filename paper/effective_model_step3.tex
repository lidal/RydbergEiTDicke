\large the adiabatic elimination, done explicitly}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
A companion note reduces the four-level cavity Rydberg-EIT problem all the way to a Dicke
model. Here we stop two steps earlier, at the three-level cavity Hamiltonian
$H^{(3)}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:core}, and spend the space instead on Step~3 --- the adiabatic
elimination of the intermediate state $\ket{2}$ --- which is where the Raman coupling
$\lambda=g_0\Om_c/2\Delta$, the light shifts, and the residual spontaneous emission all come
from. Doing it explicitly pays for itself three times: the rank-one structure of the coupling
shows that $\lambda$ and the dominant systematic cannot be tuned independently; the same
algebra returns the error of the reduction with no fitted constant, verified here against exact
diagonalisation; and the invariant $\lambda^2/\gamma_3=g_0^2/\Gamma_e$ shows that neither the
detuning nor the control power improves the sensor, only atom number and cavity finesse.
Throughout $\hbar=1$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact Hamiltonian}

Four atomic levels with laboratory energies $\omega_1=0<\omega_2<\omega_3<\omega_4$:
\[
  \ket{1}=\ket{5S_{1/2}},\quad \ket{2}=\ket{5P_{3/2}},\quad
  \ket{3}=\ket{53D_{5/2}},\quad \ket{4}=\ket{54P_{3/2}},
\]
numerically $\omega_2/2\pi=384.25$, $\omega_3/2\pi=1008.82$~THz, and
$(\omega_4-\omega_3)/2\pi\simeq10$~GHz. Writing $\sigma_{jk}=\ket{j}\bra{k}$, and with no
approximations whatsoever,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  H = {}& \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{4}\omega_j\,\sigma_{jj} + \omega_{\rm cav}\,a\dg a}_{\text{free}}
   + \underbrace{g_0\,(a+a\dg)(\sigma_{12}+\sigma_{21})}_{\text{cavity, }\ket{1}\leftrightarrow\ket{2}} \\[2ex]
   & + \underbrace{\Om_c\cos(\nu_c t)\,(\sigma_{23}+\sigma_{32})}_{\text{control laser}}
   + \underbrace{\Omu\cos(\nu_\mu t)\,(\sigma_{34}+\sigma_{43})}_{\text{microwave}} .
\end{split}
  \label{eq:lab}
\end{equation}
The classical fields carry their full time dependence; the cavity coupling carries both
$a$ and $a\dg$. Nothing has been dropped.

\section{Step 1: the rotating frame}

Transform with $\tilde H = U H U\dg + i\dot U U\dg$, choosing
\begin{equation}
  U(t)=\exp\Bigl\{i t\bigl[\omega_L\, a\dg a + \omega_L\,\sigma_{22}
        + (\omega_L+\nu_c)\,\sigma_{33} + (\omega_L+\nu_c+\nu_\mu)\,\sigma_{44}\bigr]\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ each level is rotated at the \emph{sum of the drive frequencies needed to reach it}
($\omega_L$ is a reference near the cavity resonance). This is an exact change of picture. Its
whole effect is on the diagonal:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
level & rotated at & lab energy & rotating-frame energy \\
\midrule
$\ket{1}$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$\\
$\ket{2}$ & $\omega_L$ & $2\pi\!\times\!384$~THz & $\Delta \equiv \omega_2-\omega_L$ \quad($\sim$GHz)\\
$\ket{3}$ & $\omega_L+\nu_c$ & $2\pi\!\times\!1009$~THz & $\delta \equiv \omega_3-\omega_L-\nu_c$ \quad($\sim$MHz)\\
$\ket{4}$ & $\omega_L+\nu_c+\nu_\mu$ & $\omega_3+2\pi\!\times\!10$~GHz &
   $\delta+\Dmu$, \ \ $\Dmu \equiv (\omega_4-\omega_3)-\nu_\mu$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{This answers the obvious objection.} $\ket{3}$ and $\ket{4}$ are \emph{not} degenerate
--- they are $10$~GHz apart. But the frame rotates $\ket{4}$ faster than $\ket{3}$ by exactly
$\nu_\mu$, and $\nu_\mu$ is chosen close to that splitting. The $10$~GHz is subtracted off;
what remains on the diagonal is only the residual \emph{microwave detuning} $\Dmu$, of order
MHz. Setting $\Dmu=0$ makes the two entries equal --- and that is a statement about the
\emph{drive}, not about the atom.
\end{keybox}

\section{Step 2: the rotating-wave approximation}

In the new frame the cavity term $a\dg\sigma_{21}$ picks up $e^{2i\omega_L t}$, and
$\cos(\nu t)\sigma_{jk}$ splits into a static piece and one at $e^{2i\nu t}$. Dropping the
fast pieces requires
\begin{equation}
  g_0,\ \Om_c,\ \Omu \ \ll\ \omega_L,\ \nu_c,\ \nu_\mu ,
  \tag{A1}
\end{equation}
which is spectacularly well satisfied (MHz against THz, and MHz against the $10$~GHz microwave).
The result is time-independent and still exact to this order:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde H = \omega_c\, a\dg a +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0        & g_0 a\dg & 0        & 0 \\[2pt]
    g_0 a    & \Delta   & \Om_c/2  & 0 \\[2pt]
    0        & \Om_c/2  & \delta   & \Omu/2 \\[2pt]
    0        & 0        & \Omu/2   & \delta+\Dmu
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \omega_c \equiv \omega_{\rm cav}-\omega_L .
  \label{eq:H4}
\end{equation}
Note the matrix is tridiagonal: each field connects only neighbouring levels.

\section{Step 3: eliminate the intermediate state, in full}

This is the step that does the real work, so we do it slowly. Nothing below is new physics;
the point is that every factor in $\lambda=g_0\Om_c/2\Delta$ can be traced, and that the same
algebra hands us the size of the error and the price paid in spontaneous emission.

\subsection{Partition the space}

Split the four levels into the ones we want to keep and the one we want gone,
\begin{equation}
  P = \sigma_{11}+\sigma_{33}+\sigma_{44}, \qquad Q = \sigma_{22},
  \qquad P+Q=\mathbf{1}, \qquad PQ=0 .
\end{equation}
Reordering Eq.~\eqref{eq:H4} as $(\ket{1},\ket{3},\ket{4}\,;\,\ket{2})$ puts it in block form
\begin{equation}
  \tilde H = \omega_c\,a\dg a
  + \begin{pmatrix} H_{P} & V \\[2pt] V\dg & \Delta \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  H_P=\begin{pmatrix} 0&0&0\\ 0&\delta&\Omu/2\\ 0&\Omu/2&\delta+\Dmu\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  V=\begin{pmatrix} g_0\,a\dg \\[2pt] \Om_c/2 \\[2pt] 0\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:block}
\end{equation}
$V$ is a $3\times1$ column whose entries are cavity operators: it is the only thing that
connects the two blocks, and $\ket{4}$ does not appear in it at all --- the microwave never
touches $\ket{2}$. Everything that follows is built from $V$.

\subsection{The elimination}

Expand the state as $\ket{\Psi}=\sum_j \ket{j}\otimes\ket{\phi_j}$, where the $\ket{\phi_j}$ are
(unnormalised) cavity states. Reading the rows of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H4} off directly,
\begin{align}
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_1} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_1} + g_0\,a\dg\ket{\phi_2},
    \label{eq:c1}\\
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_2} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_2}
     + \Delta\ket{\phi_2} + g_0\,a\ket{\phi_1} + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_3},
    \label{eq:c2}\\
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_3} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_3}
     + \delta\ket{\phi_3} + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_2} + \tfrac{\Omu}{2}\ket{\phi_4},
    \label{eq:c3}\\
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_4} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_4}
     + (\delta+\Dmu)\ket{\phi_4} + \tfrac{\Omu}{2}\ket{\phi_3}.
\end{align}
Only Eq.~\eqref{eq:c2} has a large coefficient. Move to the frame that removes the slow
diagonal terms, $\ket{\phi_j}=e^{-i\omega_c a\dg a\,t}e^{-i\epsilon_j t}\ket{\chi_j}$ with
$\epsilon_{1,3,4}=0,\delta,\delta+\Dmu$. Equation~\eqref{eq:c2} becomes
\begin{equation}
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\chi_2} = \Delta\ket{\chi_2}
   + \underbrace{g_0\,a\,e^{-i\omega_c t}\ket{\chi_1}
     + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}e^{-i\delta t}\ket{\chi_3}}_{\textstyle S(t)} ,
\end{equation}
a driven first-order equation whose source $S(t)$ has amplitude $\sim g_0\sqrt{\langle n\rangle},
\Om_c/2$ and turns over at rates $\omega_c,\delta$. If $|\Delta|$ dominates all four of those,
$\ket{\chi_2}$ has no time to build up: it locks to the instantaneous value that makes the
right-hand side vanish, $\ket{\chi_2}\simeq -S(t)/\Delta$. Back in the original frame,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \ket{\phi_2} \;\simeq\; -\frac{1}{\Delta}
    \Bigl(g_0\,a\ket{\phi_1} + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_3}\Bigr)\ }
  \label{eq:slave}
\end{equation}
$\ket{2}$ is no longer a degree of freedom; it is a \emph{shadow} of the two states that drive
it. Substitute Eq.~\eqref{eq:slave} wherever $\ket{\phi_2}$ appears. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:c1},
\begin{equation}
  g_0\,a\dg\ket{\phi_2} = -\frac{g_0^2}{\Delta}\,a\dg a\ket{\phi_1}
      - \frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\,a\dg\ket{\phi_3},
\end{equation}
and in Eq.~\eqref{eq:c3},
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_2} = -\frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\,a\ket{\phi_1}
      - \frac{\Om_c^{2}}{4\Delta}\ket{\phi_3}.
\end{equation}
Two terms of each kind, four in all, and they assemble into a $3\times3$ matrix in
$\{\ket{1},\ket{3},\ket{4}\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde H^{(3)} = \omega_c a\dg a +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    -\dfrac{g_0^2 a\dg a}{\Delta} & -\lambda\,a\dg & 0 \\[9pt]
    -\lambda\,a & \delta - \dfrac{\Om_c^2}{4\Delta} & \Omu/2 \\[9pt]
    0 & \Omu/2 & \delta+\Dmu
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \boxed{\ \lambda = \frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\ }
  \label{eq:H3}
\end{equation}
Note that the $\ket{1}$ shift came out \emph{normally ordered}, $a\dg a$ and not $a a\dg$: the
photon is absorbed before it is re-emitted, so an empty cavity is not shifted. Had we eliminated
$\ket{2}$ carelessly we might have written $n+1$ where the correct answer is $n$.

\subsection{The same result in one line}

Equation~\eqref{eq:H3} is the second-order term of the Feshbach (Schur-complement) reduction of
the block matrix~\eqref{eq:block},
\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm eff}(E) = H_P + V\,(E-\Delta)^{-1}\,V\dg
    \;\xrightarrow[\ E\to0\ ]{}\; H_P - \frac{V V\dg}{\Delta},
  \label{eq:feshbach}
\end{equation}
and the outer product does the bookkeeping for us in one step:
\begin{equation}
  V V\dg =
  \begin{pmatrix} g_0 a\dg \\ \Om_c/2 \\ 0\end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} g_0 a & \Om_c/2 & 0\end{pmatrix}
  =\begin{pmatrix}
     g_0^2\,a\dg a & \tfrac{g_0\Om_c}{2}a\dg & 0\\[3pt]
     \tfrac{g_0\Om_c}{2}a & \Om_c^2/4 & 0\\[3pt]
     0&0&0
   \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:VVdag}
\end{equation}
Divide by $-\Delta$ and add $H_P$: Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3}, identically. Rank one, which is why the
Raman coupling is not independent of the two light shifts --- a fact we return to below.

\subsection{Reading the three new terms}

\paragraph{$-\lambda(a\dg\sigma_{13}+a\,\sigma_{31})$, the Raman coupling.} A cavity photon is
absorbed on $\ket{1}\!\to\!\ket{2}$ and a control photon is emitted on $\ket{2}\!\to\!\ket{3}$,
with $\ket{2}$ visited only virtually. The product of the two matrix elements divided by the
energy denominator is $g_0\!\cdot\!(\Om_c/2)/\Delta$. This is the term the whole scheme is
built on, and it is \emph{first} order in each field: halving the control power halves
$\lambda$. The overall minus sign is a phase convention ($\ket{3}\to-\ket{3}$ removes it) and we
drop it from here on.

\paragraph{$-\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ on $\ket{3}$, the control light shift.} Absorbed by redefining the
two-photon detuning, $\delta\to\delta-\Om_c^2/4\Delta$. Harmless as bookkeeping, not harmless as
physics --- see the box at the end of this section.

\paragraph{$-(g_0^2/\Delta)\,a\dg a$ on $\ket{1}$, the dispersive shift.} This one multiplies
$a\dg a$, so it is not an atomic energy at all but a shift of the \emph{cavity}. With $N$ atoms
sitting in $\ket{1}$ it adds up:
\begin{equation}
  \omega_c \;\longrightarrow\; \omega_c - \frac{N g_0^2}{\Delta}
  \;=\; \omega_c\Bigl(1 - \frac{N g_0^2}{\Delta\,\omega_c}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
For $N=10^6$, $g_0/2\pi=1$~MHz and $\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz this is $2\pi\times10$~GHz, vastly
larger than $\omega_c/2\pi\sim1$~MHz. It is not a perturbation to be neglected; it is a
redefinition of where the cavity sits, and in the laboratory it is absorbed by locking the
cavity to the shifted resonance. What matters for the sensor is only that it drifts with $N$
and with $\Delta$.

\subsection{Keeping the loss}

State $\ket{2}$ is not merely inconvenient, it radiates at $\Gamma_e/2\pi\simeq6$~MHz. Nothing
above changes if we track that by letting the denominator go complex,
$\Delta\to\Delta-i\Gamma_e/2$, which is the standard non-Hermitian bookkeeping for a level whose
population leaves the system. Expanding for $|\Delta|\gg\Gamma_e$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{\Delta-i\Gamma_e/2} \simeq \frac{1}{\Delta} + \frac{i\Gamma_e}{2\Delta^2},
\end{equation}
so each entry of Eq.~\eqref{eq:VVdag} acquires an imaginary part. Two of them are the price of
the elimination:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \gamma_3 = \frac{\Om_c^2}{4\Delta^2}\,\Gamma_e
  \quad(\text{Rydberg state}), \qquad
  \kappa_{\rm abs} = \frac{g_0^2}{\Delta^2}\,\Gamma_e \quad(\text{cavity})\ }
  \label{eq:losses}
\end{equation}
Both are $\Gamma_e$ multiplied by the residual $\ket{2}$ population that the corresponding drive
maintains, $(\Om_c/2\Delta)^2$ and $(g_0/\Delta)^2$ per intracavity photon respectively; the elimination has not
removed the decay, only diluted it. At $\Om_c/2\pi=5$~MHz, $g_0/2\pi=1$~MHz,
$\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz these are $2\pi\times3.75$ and $0.60$~kHz, confirmed against the exact
eigenvalues to better than $0.5\%$.

The scaling looks at first like a free lunch --- $\lambda\propto\Delta^{-1}$ but
$\gamma_3\propto\Delta^{-2}$, so detuning further seems to buy coherence faster than it costs
coupling. It does not. Form the combination in which the $\Delta$'s and $\Om_c$'s cancel:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\lambda^{2}}{\gamma_3}
  = \frac{g_0^2\Om_c^2/4\Delta^2}{\Om_c^2\Gamma_e/4\Delta^2}
  = \frac{g_0^2}{\Gamma_e}\ }
  \label{eq:invariant}
\end{equation}
independent of \emph{both} knobs the elimination gives us. Detuning further and raising the
control power to compensate slides $\lambda$ and $\gamma_3$ along a line of constant
$\lambda^2/\gamma_3$; Step~3 provides no leverage on it whatsoever. Divide by the cavity
linewidth and, with the collective enhancement $\lambda\to\lambda\sqrt N$ at fixed single-atom
$\gamma_3$, this is the familiar figure of merit and the only one in the problem:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{N\lambda^{2}}{\kappa\,\gamma_3} = \frac{N g_0^{2}}{\kappa\,\Gamma_e} = NC .
\end{equation}
Atom number and cavity finesse are what improve the sensor; $\Delta$ and $\Om_c$ only decide
where on the line one sits. The cavity absorption is the exception that proves the point ---
$\lambda^2/\kappa_{\rm abs}=\Om_c^2/4\Gamma_e$ \emph{does} reward control power, because
$\kappa_{\rm abs}$ has no $\Om_c$ in it.

\subsection{How good is it?}

The one thing dropped between Eqs.~\eqref{eq:feshbach} and~\eqref{eq:H3} is the energy
dependence of the resolvent. Restoring it,
\begin{equation}
  (E-\Delta)^{-1} = -\frac{1}{\Delta}\Bigl(1+\frac{E}{\Delta}+\dots\Bigr)
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \boxed{\ \delta E \simeq \frac{E\,\langle V V\dg\rangle}{\Delta^{2}},
  \qquad
  \langle VV\dg\rangle = g_0^2\langle a\dg a\rangle P_1 + \frac{\Om_c^2}{4}P_3\ }
  \label{eq:err}
\end{equation}
with $P_1,P_3$ the populations of $\ket{1},\ket{3}$ in the state whose energy is $E$. Two
things are worth noticing. The error is second order in $1/\Delta$ while $\lambda$ is only
first order, so the model gets \emph{relatively} better as $\Delta$ grows even though the
physics it describes gets weaker. And a state with no $\ket{3}$ component --- a photon with the
atom in $\ket{1}$ --- is described far more accurately than the dressed states, since it is
only $g_0$, not $\Om_c$, that reaches $\ket{2}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:adiabatic} tests both statements against exact diagonalisation. The
rotating-wave Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:H4} conserves
$N_{\rm exc}=a\dg a+\sigma_{22}+\sigma_{33}+\sigma_{44}$, so its single-excitation sector
$(\ket{1,n{=}1},\ket{2,0},\ket{3,0},\ket{4,0})$ is a closed $4\times4$ problem and the
comparison needs no truncation. Panel~(a): Eq.~\eqref{eq:err} predicts the error with no
adjustable constant, over two decades. Panel~(b): the avoided crossing between the cavity and
the lower dressed branch, whose width is the quantity Step~3 exists to produce.

\subsection{When is it allowed?}

Collecting the conditions used above,
\begin{equation}
  |\Delta| \ \gg\ \Om_c,\quad g_0\sqrt{\langle a\dg a\rangle},\quad
  \Omu,\quad |\delta|,\quad |\omega_c|,\quad \Gamma_e .
  \tag{A2}
\end{equation}
The first two are the amplitudes of the source $S(t)$, the next three its turnover rates, and
the last is the width of the state being eliminated. It is the \emph{full} list: a treatment
that checks only $|\Delta|\gg\Om_c,\Gamma_e$ will be caught out at high intracavity photon
number, where $g_0\sqrt{\langle n\rangle}$ creeps up on $\Delta$. With
$\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz and $g_0/2\pi=1$~MHz the elimination survives to
$\langle n\rangle\sim10^3$ and no further.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Where the dominant systematic comes from.} $VV\dg$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:VVdag} has rank one,
so the Raman coupling and the light shift are not independent knobs: $\lambda\propto\Om_c$ and
the shift $\propto\Om_c^2$ both track the control-laser intensity, and the shift adds
\emph{directly} to $\delta$ --- the same channel the measurand uses. Since $\Om_c^2\propto I$, a
fractional intensity fluctuation $\varepsilon$ moves $\delta$ by
$\varepsilon\,\Om_c^2/4\Delta$, at our numbers $\varepsilon\times2\pi\times62$~kHz. Control-laser intensity noise is therefore
structurally indistinguishable from signal, and no amount of care with the microwave will fix
it.
\end{keybox}
\section{Step 4: dress the Rydberg pair}

The lower-right block of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3} is a two-level problem on its own:
\begin{equation}
  H_\mu=\begin{pmatrix}\delta & \Omu/2\\ \Omu/2 & \delta+\Dmu\end{pmatrix}
      = \Bigl(\delta+\tfrac{\Dmu}{2}\Bigr)\mathbf{1}
        + \tfrac12\bigl(\Omu\,\sigma_x + \Dmu\,\sigma_z\bigr).
\end{equation}
Diagonalise with the rotation $R(\theta)$ defined by
\begin{equation}
  \tan\theta = \frac{\Omu}{\Dmu},
  \qquad
  \ket{+} = \sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{3}+\cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{4},
  \qquad
  \ket{-} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{3}-\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{4},
\end{equation}
giving the \emph{generalised Rabi} splitting
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \omega_\pm=\delta+\frac{\Dmu}{2}\pm\frac{W}{2},
  \qquad W=\sqrt{\Omu^{2}+\Dmu^{2}}\ }
  \label{eq:branches}
\end{equation}
Now rotate the rest of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3}. The Raman coupling connects $\ket{1}$ to the
\emph{bare} $\ket{3}=\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{+}+\cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{-}$, so it splits between
the branches in that ratio. In the basis $(\ket{g},\ket{+},\ket{-})$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  H^{(3)}=\begin{pmatrix}
    0 & g_+ a\dg & g_- a\dg\\[3pt]
    g_+ a & \omega_+ & 0\\[3pt]
    g_- a & 0 & \omega_-
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  g_+=\lambda\sin\tfrac\theta2,\quad g_-=\lambda\cos\tfrac\theta2 \;}
  \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
There is no $\ket{+}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{-}$ entry: that transition is the microwave one and
carries no cavity matrix element.

Equation~\eqref{eq:core} is where this note stops. It is still excitation-number conserving,
so on its own it radiates nothing; turning it into a Dicke model requires counter-rotating
terms $a\dg b\dg+ab$ from a second drive tone, and that, together with the
Holstein--Primakoff step and the superradiant threshold, is the subject of the companion note.
