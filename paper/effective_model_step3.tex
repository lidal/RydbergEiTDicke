\title{\bfseries From four levels to the Dicke model\\[3pt]
\large the adiabatic elimination, done explicitly}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
A companion note reduces the four-level cavity Rydberg-EIT problem all the way to a Dicke
model. Here we stop two steps earlier, at the three-level cavity Hamiltonian $H^{(3)}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:core}, and spend the space instead on Step~3 --- the elimination of the
intermediate state $\ket{2}$, which is where the Raman coupling $\lambda=g_0\Om_c/2\Delta$, the
light shifts and the residual spontaneous emission all come from. One line of second-order
perturbation theory does it, $H^{\rm eff}_{mn}=\tilde H_{mn}-V_mV_n\dg/\Delta$, and that one
line pays for itself three times: its rank-one structure shows that $\lambda$ and the dominant
systematic cannot be tuned independently; putting $\Gamma_e$ in the denominator returns the
inherited decay rates and the invariant $\lambda^2/\gamma_3=g_0^2/\Gamma_e$, which says neither
the detuning nor the control power improves the sensor; and keeping the next order returns the
error of the reduction, verified against exact diagonalisation with no fitted constant.
Throughout $\hbar=1$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact Hamiltonian}

Four atomic levels with laboratory energies $\omega_1=0<\omega_2<\omega_3<\omega_4$:
\[
  \ket{1}=\ket{5S_{1/2}},\quad \ket{2}=\ket{5P_{3/2}},\quad
  \ket{3}=\ket{53D_{5/2}},\quad \ket{4}=\ket{54P_{3/2}},
\]
numerically $\omega_2/2\pi=384.25$, $\omega_3/2\pi=1008.82$~THz, and
$(\omega_4-\omega_3)/2\pi\simeq10$~GHz. Writing $\sigma_{jk}=\ket{j}\bra{k}$, and with no
approximations whatsoever,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  H = {}& \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{4}\omega_j\,\sigma_{jj} + \omega_{\rm cav}\,a\dg a}_{\text{free}}
   + \underbrace{g_0\,(a+a\dg)(\sigma_{12}+\sigma_{21})}_{\text{cavity, }\ket{1}\leftrightarrow\ket{2}} \\[2ex]
   & + \underbrace{\Om_c\cos(\nu_c t)\,(\sigma_{23}+\sigma_{32})}_{\text{control laser}}
   + \underbrace{\Omu\cos(\nu_\mu t)\,(\sigma_{34}+\sigma_{43})}_{\text{microwave}} .
\end{split}
  \label{eq:lab}
\end{equation}
The classical fields carry their full time dependence; the cavity coupling carries both
$a$ and $a\dg$. Nothing has been dropped.

\section{Step 1: the rotating frame}

Transform with $\tilde H = U H U\dg + i\dot U U\dg$, choosing
\begin{equation}
  U(t)=\exp\Bigl\{i t\bigl[\omega_L\, a\dg a + \omega_L\,\sigma_{22}
        + (\omega_L+\nu_c)\,\sigma_{33} + (\omega_L+\nu_c+\nu_\mu)\,\sigma_{44}\bigr]\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ each level is rotated at the \emph{sum of the drive frequencies needed to reach it}
($\omega_L$ is a reference near the cavity resonance). This is an exact change of picture. Its
whole effect is on the diagonal:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
level & rotated at & lab energy & rotating-frame energy \\
\midrule
$\ket{1}$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$\\
$\ket{2}$ & $\omega_L$ & $2\pi\!\times\!384$~THz & $\Delta \equiv \omega_2-\omega_L$ \quad($\sim$GHz)\\
$\ket{3}$ & $\omega_L+\nu_c$ & $2\pi\!\times\!1009$~THz & $\delta \equiv \omega_3-\omega_L-\nu_c$ \quad($\sim$MHz)\\
$\ket{4}$ & $\omega_L+\nu_c+\nu_\mu$ & $\omega_3+2\pi\!\times\!10$~GHz &
   $\delta+\Dmu$, \ \ $\Dmu \equiv (\omega_4-\omega_3)-\nu_\mu$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{This answers the obvious objection.} $\ket{3}$ and $\ket{4}$ are \emph{not} degenerate
--- they are $10$~GHz apart. But the frame rotates $\ket{4}$ faster than $\ket{3}$ by exactly
$\nu_\mu$, and $\nu_\mu$ is chosen close to that splitting. The $10$~GHz is subtracted off;
what remains on the diagonal is only the residual \emph{microwave detuning} $\Dmu$, of order
MHz. Setting $\Dmu=0$ makes the two entries equal --- and that is a statement about the
\emph{drive}, not about the atom.
\end{keybox}

\section{Step 2: the rotating-wave approximation}

In the new frame the cavity term $a\dg\sigma_{21}$ picks up $e^{2i\omega_L t}$, and
$\cos(\nu t)\sigma_{jk}$ splits into a static piece and one at $e^{2i\nu t}$. Dropping the
fast pieces requires
\begin{equation}
  g_0,\ \Om_c,\ \Omu \ \ll\ \omega_L,\ \nu_c,\ \nu_\mu ,
  \tag{A1}
\end{equation}
which is spectacularly well satisfied (MHz against THz, and MHz against the $10$~GHz microwave).
The result is time-independent and still exact to this order:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde H = \omega_c\, a\dg a +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0        & g_0 a\dg & 0        & 0 \\[2pt]
    g_0 a    & \Delta   & \Om_c/2  & 0 \\[2pt]
    0        & \Om_c/2  & \delta   & \Omu/2 \\[2pt]
    0        & 0        & \Omu/2   & \delta+\Dmu
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \omega_c \equiv \omega_{\rm cav}-\omega_L .
  \label{eq:H4}
\end{equation}
Note the matrix is tridiagonal: each field connects only neighbouring levels.

\section{Step 3: eliminate the intermediate state}

$\ket{2}$ sits $\Delta\sim2\pi\times100$~MHz away from anything that drives it, so it is never
appreciably populated; its only job is to relay. That is precisely the situation second-order
perturbation theory was built for, and one formula does the whole step.

\subsection{One formula}

List the couplings that reach $\ket{2}$ from the states we keep. Reading the second column of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:H4},
\begin{equation}
  V_m \equiv \bra{m}\tilde H\ket{2}: \qquad
  V_1 = g_0\,a\dg, \qquad V_3 = \Om_c/2, \qquad V_4 = 0 .
  \label{eq:Vm}
\end{equation}
The $V_m$ are operators on the cavity, not numbers, and $V_4=0$ says the microwave never
touches $\ket{2}$. Quasi-degenerate second-order perturbation theory then gives the effective
Hamiltonian on $\{\ket{1},\ket{3},\ket{4}\}$ as
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ H^{\rm eff}_{mn} = \tilde H_{mn} - \frac{V_m V_n\dg}{\Delta}\ }
  \qquad m,n\in\{1,3,4\} ,
  \label{eq:pt}
\end{equation}
one term per \emph{path} $m\to2\to n$: two matrix elements over one energy denominator. (The
denominator is properly $\tfrac12[(E_m-\Delta)^{-1}+(E_n-\Delta)^{-1}]$, but $E_{1,3,4}=0,\delta,
\delta+\Dmu$ are all $\sim$MHz against $\Delta\sim100$~MHz, so $-1/\Delta$ it is; the
$\delta/\Delta$ correction is smaller than the error we quantify in \S\ref{sec:err}.)

Four paths are non-zero, and evaluating them is the entire step:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
path & $V_mV_n\dg$ & contributes & is \\
\midrule
$1\to2\to1$ & $g_0a\dg\cdot g_0a=g_0^2\,a\dg a$ & $-g_0^2 a\dg a/\Delta$ & a cavity shift\\
$3\to2\to3$ & $\Om_c^2/4$ & $-\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ & a light shift on $\ket{3}$\\
$1\to2\to3$ & $g_0a\dg\cdot\Om_c/2$ & $-\lambda\,a\dg$ & \textbf{the Raman coupling}\\
$3\to2\to1$ & $\Om_c/2\cdot g_0 a$ & $-\lambda\,a$ & its conjugate\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{2pt}
Anything involving $\ket{4}$ vanishes because $V_4=0$. Assembling them,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde H^{(3)} = \omega_c a\dg a +
  \begin{pmatrix}
    -\dfrac{g_0^2 a\dg a}{\Delta} & -\lambda\,a\dg & 0 \\[9pt]
    -\lambda\,a & \delta - \dfrac{\Om_c^2}{4\Delta} & \Omu/2 \\[9pt]
    0 & \Omu/2 & \delta+\Dmu
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \boxed{\ \lambda = \frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\ }
  \label{eq:H3}
\end{equation}
That is Step~3. Two features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pt} are worth pausing on, because both have
consequences later. It is an \emph{outer product}, $V_mV_n\dg$, hence rank one --- the two
light shifts and the Raman coupling are three views of one object, not three knobs. And the
operator ordering came out right on its own: $V_1V_1\dg=g_0a\dg\,g_0a\propto a\dg a$, normally
ordered, so an empty cavity receives no shift. Guessing the answer rather than computing it
risks an $n+1$ where the truth is $n$.

\subsection{This is the rotating-wave approximation again}

It is worth saying plainly, because it makes Step~3 an application of Step~2 rather than a new
idea. In both steps something oscillates fast compared with everything driving it: in Step~2 the
counter-rotating field at $2\nu$, here the amplitude of $\ket{2}$ at $\Delta$. In both, the fast
thing averages to zero at first order and leaves a second-order residue of size
$(\text{amplitude})^2/(\text{frequency})$ --- which is exactly what Eq.~\eqref{eq:pt} computes,
with $V$ the amplitude and $\Delta$ the frequency. The validity conditions (A1) and (A2) are the
same statement twice. Appendix~\ref{app:hand} does the same elimination on the amplitude
equations for anyone who wants to watch $\ket{2}$ become a shadow of the states that drive it.

Equation~\eqref{eq:pt} is also the leading term of something exact. Keeping the energy $E$ of
the state one is after, rather than setting it to zero, the reduction
$H^{\rm eff}_{mn}(E)=\tilde H_{mn}+V_m(E-\Delta)^{-1}V_n\dg$ is exact to all orders; expanding
$(E-\Delta)^{-1}$ in $E/\Delta$ returns Eq.~\eqref{eq:pt} at leading order and, at the next one,
the error quantified in \S\ref{sec:err}.

\subsection{Reading the three terms}

\paragraph{$-\lambda(a\dg\sigma_{13}+a\,\sigma_{31})$, the Raman coupling.} A cavity photon is
absorbed on $\ket{1}\!\to\!\ket{2}$ and a control photon emitted on $\ket{2}\!\to\!\ket{3}$,
with $\ket{2}$ visited only virtually. It is the term the whole scheme is built on, and it is
\emph{first} order in each field: halving the control power halves $\lambda$. The minus sign is
a phase convention ($\ket{3}\to-\ket{3}$ removes it) and we drop it.

\paragraph{$-\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ on $\ket{3}$, the control light shift.} Absorbed by redefining
$\delta\to\delta-\Om_c^2/4\Delta$. Harmless as bookkeeping, not harmless as physics --- see the
box at the end of this section.

\paragraph{$-(g_0^2/\Delta)\,a\dg a$ on $\ket{1}$, the dispersive shift.} This multiplies
$a\dg a$, so it is not an atomic energy at all but a shift of the \emph{cavity}, and with $N$
atoms in $\ket{1}$ it adds up: $\omega_c\to\omega_c-Ng_0^2/\Delta$. For $N=10^6$,
$g_0/2\pi=1$~MHz, $\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz that is $2\pi\times10$~GHz against $\omega_c/2\pi\sim
1$~MHz. It is not a perturbation to be dropped but a redefinition of where the cavity sits,
absorbed in the laboratory by locking to the shifted resonance. What matters for the sensor is
that it drifts with $N$ and with $\Delta$.

\subsection{Keeping the loss}

$\ket{2}$ radiates at $\Gamma_e/2\pi\simeq6$~MHz. Nothing above changes if we let the
denominator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pt} go complex, $\Delta\to\Delta-i\Gamma_e/2$, the standard
bookkeeping for a level whose population leaves the system. Expanding
$(\Delta-i\Gamma_e/2)^{-1}\simeq\Delta^{-1}+i\Gamma_e/2\Delta^2$, the two diagonal paths acquire
imaginary parts:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \gamma_3 = \frac{\Om_c^2}{4\Delta^2}\,\Gamma_e
  \quad(\text{Rydberg state}), \qquad
  \kappa_{\rm abs} = \frac{g_0^2}{\Delta^2}\,\Gamma_e \quad(\text{cavity})\ }
  \label{eq:losses}
\end{equation}
Each is $\Gamma_e$ times the residual $\ket{2}$ population the corresponding drive maintains,
$(\Om_c/2\Delta)^2$ and $(g_0/\Delta)^2$ per intracavity photon: the elimination has not removed
the decay, only diluted it. At $\Om_c/2\pi=5$, $g_0/2\pi=1$, $\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz these are
$2\pi\times3.75$ and $0.60$~kHz, confirmed against the exact eigenvalues to better than $0.5\%$.

The scaling looks at first like a free lunch --- $\lambda\propto\Delta^{-1}$ but
$\gamma_3\propto\Delta^{-2}$, so detuning further seems to buy coherence faster than it costs
coupling. It does not. Form the combination in which $\Delta$ and $\Om_c$ both cancel:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\lambda^{2}}{\gamma_3}
  = \frac{g_0^2\Om_c^2/4\Delta^2}{\Om_c^2\Gamma_e/4\Delta^2}
  = \frac{g_0^2}{\Gamma_e}\ }
  \label{eq:invariant}
\end{equation}
independent of \emph{both} knobs Step~3 gives us. Detuning further and raising the control power
to compensate slides $\lambda$ and $\gamma_3$ along a line of constant $\lambda^2/\gamma_3$.
Divide by the cavity linewidth and, with $\lambda\to\lambda\sqrt N$ at fixed single-atom
$\gamma_3$, this is the familiar figure of merit and the only one in the problem,
$N\lambda^2/\kappa\gamma_3 = Ng_0^2/\kappa\Gamma_e = NC$. Atom number and cavity finesse improve
the sensor; $\Delta$ and $\Om_c$ only decide where on the line one sits. The cavity absorption
is the exception that proves the point --- $\lambda^2/\kappa_{\rm abs}=\Om_c^2/4\Gamma_e$
\emph{does} reward control power, because $\kappa_{\rm abs}$ has no $\Om_c$ in it.

\subsection{How good is it, and when is it allowed?}
\label{sec:err}

Restoring the energy dependence of the resolvent,
$(E-\Delta)^{-1}=-\Delta^{-1}(1+E/\Delta+\dots)$, gives the leading error directly:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \delta E \simeq \frac{E\,\langle V V\dg\rangle}{\Delta^{2}},
  \qquad
  \langle VV\dg\rangle = g_0^2\langle a\dg a\rangle P_1 + \frac{\Om_c^2}{4}P_3\ }
  \label{eq:err}
\end{equation}
with $P_1,P_3$ the populations of $\ket{1},\ket{3}$ in the state whose energy is $E$. The error
is second order in $1/\Delta$ while $\lambda$ is only first order, so the model gets
\emph{relatively} better as $\Delta$ grows even though the physics it describes gets weaker; and
a state with no $\ket{3}$ component is described far more accurately than the dressed states,
since only $g_0$, not $\Om_c$, reaches $\ket{2}$ from $\ket{1}$.

Figure~\ref{fig:adiabatic} tests both statements. Equation~\eqref{eq:H4} conserves
$N_{\rm exc}=a\dg a+\sigma_{22}+\sigma_{33}+\sigma_{44}$, so its single-excitation sector
$(\ket{1,n{=}1},\ket{2,0},\ket{3,0},\ket{4,0})$ is a closed $4\times4$ problem and the
comparison needs no truncation.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_adiabatic.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Step 3 tested against exact diagonalisation}, in the single-excitation
  sector at $g_0/2\pi=1$, $\Om_c/2\pi=5$, $\Omu/2\pi=2$, $\delta/2\pi=0.3$~MHz, $\Dmu=0$.
  \textbf{(a)} Worst-case eigenvalue error versus $\Delta$ (points) against
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:err} with no fitted parameter (line); the Raman coupling $\lambda$ is shown for
  scale, falling only as $\Delta^{-1}$. \textbf{(b)} The avoided crossing at
  $\Delta/2\pi=100$~MHz. Measured minimum gap $35.9$~kHz against the predicted
  $2g_-=\sqrt2\,\lambda=35.4$~kHz; the $1.5\%$ excess is the $\Om_c^2/4\Delta$ light shift,
  which sits on $\ket{3}$ alone and so slightly reshapes the dressing of Step~4.}
  \label{fig:adiabatic}
\end{figure}

Collecting the conditions,
\begin{equation}
  |\Delta| \ \gg\ \Om_c,\quad g_0\sqrt{\langle a\dg a\rangle},\quad
  \Omu,\quad |\delta|,\quad |\omega_c|,\quad \Gamma_e .
  \tag{A2}
\end{equation}
The first two are the amplitudes reaching $\ket{2}$, the next three the rates at which they
turn over, the last the width of the state being eliminated. It is the \emph{full} list: a
treatment that checks only $|\Delta|\gg\Om_c,\Gamma_e$ is caught out at high intracavity photon
number, where $g_0\sqrt{\langle n\rangle}$ creeps up on $\Delta$. At $\Delta/2\pi=100$ and
$g_0/2\pi=1$~MHz the elimination survives to $\langle n\rangle\sim10^3$ and no further.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Where the dominant systematic comes from.} $V_mV_n\dg$ has rank one, so the Raman
coupling and the light shift are not independent knobs: $\lambda\propto\Om_c$ and the shift
$\propto\Om_c^2$ both track the control-laser intensity, and the shift adds \emph{directly} to
$\delta$ --- the same channel the measurand uses. Since $\Om_c^2\propto I$, a fractional
intensity fluctuation $\varepsilon$ moves $\delta$ by $\varepsilon\,\Om_c^2/4\Delta$, at our
numbers $\varepsilon\times2\pi\times62$~kHz. Control-laser intensity noise is therefore
structurally indistinguishable from signal, and no amount of care with the microwave will fix
it.
\end{keybox}

\section{Step 4: dress the Rydberg pair}

The lower-right block of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3} is a two-level problem on its own:
\begin{equation}
  H_\mu=\begin{pmatrix}\delta & \Omu/2\\ \Omu/2 & \delta+\Dmu\end{pmatrix}
      = \Bigl(\delta+\tfrac{\Dmu}{2}\Bigr)\mathbf{1}
        + \tfrac12\bigl(\Omu\,\sigma_x + \Dmu\,\sigma_z\bigr).
\end{equation}
Diagonalise with the rotation $R(\theta)$ defined by
\begin{equation}
  \tan\theta = \frac{\Omu}{\Dmu},
  \qquad
  \ket{+} = \sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{3}+\cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{4},
  \qquad
  \ket{-} = \cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{3}-\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{4},
\end{equation}
giving the \emph{generalised Rabi} splitting
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \omega_\pm=\delta+\frac{\Dmu}{2}\pm\frac{W}{2},
  \qquad W=\sqrt{\Omu^{2}+\Dmu^{2}}\ }
  \label{eq:branches}
\end{equation}
Now rotate the rest of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3}. The Raman coupling connects $\ket{1}$ to the
\emph{bare} $\ket{3}=\sin\tfrac\theta2\ket{+}+\cos\tfrac\theta2\ket{-}$, so it splits between
the branches in that ratio. In the basis $(\ket{g},\ket{+},\ket{-})$:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;
  H^{(3)}=\begin{pmatrix}
    0 & g_+ a\dg & g_- a\dg\\[3pt]
    g_+ a & \omega_+ & 0\\[3pt]
    g_- a & 0 & \omega_-
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  g_+=\lambda\sin\tfrac\theta2,\quad g_-=\lambda\cos\tfrac\theta2 \;}
  \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
There is no $\ket{+}\!\leftrightarrow\!\ket{-}$ entry: that transition is the microwave one and
carries no cavity matrix element.

Equation~\eqref{eq:core} is where this note stops. It is still excitation-number conserving,
so on its own it radiates nothing; turning it into a Dicke model requires counter-rotating
terms $a\dg b\dg+ab$ from a second drive tone, and that, together with the
Holstein--Primakoff step and the superradiant threshold, is the subject of the companion note.


\appendix
\section{The same elimination by hand}
\label{app:hand}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pt} is quicker, but it is worth seeing once where it comes from. Expand the
state as $\ket{\Psi}=\sum_j\ket{j}\otimes\ket{\phi_j}$ with $\ket{\phi_j}$ (unnormalised) cavity
states. Reading the rows of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H4},
\begin{align}
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_1} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_1} + g_0\,a\dg\ket{\phi_2},
    \label{eq:c1}\\
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_2} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_2} + \Delta\ket{\phi_2}
     + g_0\,a\ket{\phi_1} + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_3},
    \label{eq:c2}\\
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\phi_3} &= \omega_c a\dg a\ket{\phi_3} + \delta\ket{\phi_3}
     + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_2} + \tfrac{\Omu}{2}\ket{\phi_4},
    \label{eq:c3}
\end{align}
and a fourth line for $\ket{\phi_4}$ that never involves $\ket{\phi_2}$. Only
Eq.~\eqref{eq:c2} carries a large coefficient. Remove the slow diagonal terms with
$\ket{\phi_j}=e^{-i\omega_c a\dg a\,t}e^{-i\epsilon_j t}\ket{\chi_j}$,
$\epsilon_{1,3,4}=0,\delta,\delta+\Dmu$, and it becomes
\begin{equation}
  i\,\partial_t\ket{\chi_2} = \Delta\ket{\chi_2}
   + \underbrace{g_0\,a\,e^{-i\omega_c t}\ket{\chi_1}
     + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}e^{-i\delta t}\ket{\chi_3}}_{\textstyle S(t)} ,
\end{equation}
a driven first-order equation whose source has amplitudes $g_0\sqrt{\langle n\rangle}$,
$\Om_c/2$ and turns over at rates $\omega_c,\delta$ --- condition (A2), item by item. When
$|\Delta|$ dominates all of them, $\ket{\chi_2}$ never builds up: it locks to the value that
makes the right-hand side vanish, $\ket{\chi_2}\simeq-S(t)/\Delta$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  \ket{\phi_2} \;\simeq\; -\frac{1}{\Delta}
    \Bigl(g_0\,a\ket{\phi_1} + \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_3}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:slave}
\end{equation}
$\ket{2}$ is no longer a degree of freedom; it is a shadow of the two states that drive it.
Substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:c1},
\begin{equation}
  g_0\,a\dg\ket{\phi_2} = -\frac{g_0^2}{\Delta}\,a\dg a\ket{\phi_1}
      - \frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\,a\dg\ket{\phi_3},
\end{equation}
and into Eq.~\eqref{eq:c3},
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{\Om_c}{2}\ket{\phi_2} = -\frac{g_0\Om_c}{2\Delta}\,a\ket{\phi_1}
      - \frac{\Om_c^{2}}{4\Delta}\ket{\phi_3}.
\end{equation}
Four terms: the same four rows of the table in \S4.1, and the same
Eq.~\eqref{eq:H3}. The correspondence is exact --- Eq.~\eqref{eq:slave} is
$\ket{\phi_2}=-\sum_n V_n\dg\ket{\phi_n}/\Delta$, and feeding it back multiplies by $V_m$.
